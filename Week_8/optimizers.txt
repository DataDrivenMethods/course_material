Leading Commercial Solvers

  Gurobi
  - Most popular in finance for portfolio optimization, risk management, and trading strategies
  - Excellent performance on mixed-integer programming (MIP) and quadratic programming (QP)
  - Strong support for large-scale problems
  - Used for mean-variance optimization, factor models, and transaction cost optimization

  CPLEX (IBM)
  - Long-established solver, widely used in financial services
  - Strong MIP and LP capabilities
  - Common in asset allocation, portfolio rebalancing, and risk optimization
  - Good integration with enterprise systems

  MOSEK
  - Specialized in conic optimization (particularly second-order cone programming)
  - Excellent for portfolio optimization with realistic constraints
  - Handles quadratic and semidefinite programming efficiently
  - Popular for robust optimization problems

  FICO Xpress
  - Used in banking and asset management
  - Strong performance on large-scale optimization
  - Good for portfolio optimization and risk management

  Specialized Financial Software

  Bloomberg PORT (Portfolio and Risk Analytics)
  - Built on commercial solvers
  - Integrated portfolio optimization with Bloomberg data

  Axioma Portfolio Optimizer
  - Uses commercial solvers underneath
  - Factor-based portfolio construction and risk management

  MATLAB Optimization Toolbox
  - Common in quantitative finance research
  - fmincon, quadprog for portfolio problems
  - Often prototyping tool before production implementation

  Usage Patterns

  Most quantitative hedge funds, asset managers, and investment banks use Gurobi or CPLEX as their primary
  solvers. Gurobi has been gaining market share due to performance and modern API design.

  For mean-variance optimization and similar problems, MOSEK is often preferred due to its specialized conic
  optimization capabilities.

Breakdown
=========
  Gurobi

  Best for:
  - Mixed-Integer Portfolio Optimization - cardinality constraints (max N assets), buy-in thresholds, round lots
  - Index tracking with sparse portfolios - minimizing number of holdings while tracking an index
  - Transaction cost optimization - with fixed costs, minimum trade sizes
  - Multi-period portfolio optimization - with rebalancing decisions across time
  - Asset-liability management - with integer constraints on liability matching

  Problem types: LP, QP, QCQP, MIP, MIQP, MIQCQP

  Why: Industry-leading MIP performance, crucial when you have discrete decisions (buy/don't buy, position limits,
   lot sizes)

  CPLEX

  Best for:
  - Large-scale linear portfolio problems - factor-based optimization with thousands of assets
  - Network flow problems - collateral optimization, cash management
  - Scenario-based optimization - robust portfolio construction with discrete scenarios
  - Integer programming in risk management - credit portfolio optimization, CVA optimization

  Problem types: LP, QP, QCQP, MIP, MIQP, MIQCQP

  Why: Very mature, highly optimized for large-scale problems, excellent enterprise support

  MOSEK

  Best for:
  - Mean-variance optimization - classic Markowitz portfolio optimization
  - Risk parity portfolios - equalizing risk contributions (involves conic constraints)
  - Worst-case robust optimization - with ellipsoidal uncertainty sets
  - Factor risk models - quadratic risk minimization with linear constraints
  - CVaR optimization - Conditional Value at Risk minimization
  - Regularized portfolio optimization - with L2 penalties on positions

  Problem types: LP, QP, SOCP (Second-Order Cone), SDP (Semidefinite), conic optimization

  Why: Superior performance on conic problems, especially when risk is modeled as norm constraints. Natural
  formulation for many modern portfolio theory problems.

  FICO Xpress

  Best for:
  - Banking optimization - capital allocation, Basel III compliance optimization
  - ALM (Asset-Liability Management) - especially in insurance/pensions
  - Large-scale portfolio optimization - institutional asset management
  - Stochastic programming - scenario trees for multi-stage decisions

  Problem types: LP, QP, MIP, MIQP, stochastic programming

  Why: Strong in financial services sector, good stochastic programming support

  MOSEK vs Gurobi/CPLEX Decision Rule

  Use MOSEK when:
  - Pure continuous optimization (no integers)
  - Risk is your primary concern (covariance matrices, factor models)
  - Need conic constraints (||x|| ≤ t type constraints)
  - Problems like: min variance s.t. expected return ≥ target

  Use Gurobi/CPLEX when:
  - Any integer variables present
  - Cardinality constraints (max N assets, min M% per position)
  - Transaction costs with fixed components
  - Problems like: min cost s.t. tracking error ≤ ε AND ≤100 assets

  Common Finance Problem → Solver Mapping

  | Problem                       | Best Solver  | Why                     |
  |-------------------------------|--------------|-------------------------|
  | Mean-variance (continuous)    | MOSEK        | Native QP with cones    |
  | Mean-variance + max 50 stocks | Gurobi       | Integer constraints     |
  | Index tracking (sparse)       | Gurobi       | Cardinality constraints |
  | Risk parity                   | MOSEK        | Conic formulation       |
  | Multi-period rebalancing      | Gurobi/CPLEX | MIP for transactions    |
  | CVaR optimization             | MOSEK        | SOCP formulation        |
  | Factor model optimization     | MOSEK/Gurobi | QP, size-dependent      |
  | Black-Litterman               | MOSEK        | Quadratic objective     |
  | Transaction cost minimization | Gurobi       | Fixed + variable costs  |

  Many institutions use both: MOSEK for continuous risk optimization and Gurobi for discrete trading decisions.
